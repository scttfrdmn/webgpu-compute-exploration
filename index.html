WebGPU Compute Exploration

Twenty-five interactive examples of GPU computing with WebGPU compute shaders written in WGSL, from basic parallel operations to real-time physics simulations and ray-marched fractals. Each interactive example is set up as you scroll to it and runs when you press Start. Three of them are worked up as reference investigations: a question, a measurement protocol, a check against an independent reference, and predictions you can test on your own GPU.

Reference investigations

Three examples set the standard for the rest: each asks one question, sweeps it with a fixed protocol (warm-up, repeats, medians and interquartile ranges), verifies the GPU's answers against an independent CPU reference, and tests its predictions against what your GPU measures. Nothing runs until you press Run on a Measure tab.

Compute basics

1Vector Addition

Adds two vectors of one million numbers in parallel on the GPU, one invocation per element.

2Matrix Multiplication

Multiplies two 512×512 matrices with 2D workgroups, a fundamental operation in ML and graphics.

3Image Processing: Gaussian Blur

Applies a 25×25 Gaussian blur (σ = 4) to a sharp-edged test image with a compute shader.

Algorithms & signal processing

14FFT Audio Visualizer

A real-time spectrum analyzer: the Web Audio API supplies raw samples, and the GPU applies a Hann window, runs a radix-2 Cooley-Tukey FFT (one compute dispatch per stage), converts to dB, keeps a scrolling spectrogram and detects beats.

0.60 90 dB
Presets

Audio: the synth demo needs no permissions and plays quietly (Mute silences the speakers, not the analysis). The microphone is requested only when you press Microphone and is never played back. Files are decoded locally and loop. The disc in the corner flashes on detected beats.

15Parallel Sorting

Bitonic sort and LSD radix sort on the GPU. The benchmark sorts up to 4 million keys, checks the result against the CPU's sort and compares timings; the bar chart animates bitonic sort one compare-exchange stage at a time.

8

Reading the chart: background blue/purple marks blocks of size k that sort up/down in the next stage; the strip below pairs each orange bar with the cyan bar j places to its right. Gray bars are padding up to a power of two.

16K-Means Clustering

Lloyd's algorithm on up to 500,000 points: each iteration one compute pass assigns every point to its nearest centroid and a two-level workgroup reduction moves each centroid to the mean of its points. Watch the centroids travel, with trails, until no point changes cluster.

5 100k 5 5% 5

Controls: Start iterates until no point changes cluster, Step runs one iteration, Reset picks new initial centroids for the same points, Regenerate draws a new point cloud. Changing K re-initializes; changing the data sliders regenerates.

17Voronoi (Jump Flooding)

The Jump Flooding Algorithm builds a Voronoi diagram and distance field for up to 1024 seeds on a 1024×768 grid in about log2(1024) = 10 compute passes, under Euclidean, Manhattan or Chebyshev distance.

64
Layouts

Interaction: click to add a seed · drag a seed to move it · right-click or shift-click to remove the nearest seed. Animate lets the seeds drift and recomputes the diagram every frame; Exact check counts the pixels where JFA differs from brute force.

18Maze Generation & Solving

Mazes of up to 1023×767 cells are carved on the CPU, because the recursive backtracker, Kruskal's and Prim's algorithms each depend on every earlier step. A GPU then solves them with a breadth-first wavefront: one compute dispatch per distance level, one thread per cell. The flood is colored by distance, and the shortest path is traced back from the goal.

16
0%

Interaction: click to move the start (green), shift-click or right-click to move the goal (red). Loops removes a share of the walls so there are several routes and BFS has to pick the shortest; Generate builds a new maze with the selected algorithm and size.

19Cloth Simulation

A fabric of up to 128×128 particles held together by structural, shear and bend links, simulated with position-based dynamics: Verlet integration, then a constraint solver whose links are split into 12 independent color sets so each compute dispatch is race-free, then sphere and floor collisions with friction. Wind, pins, tearing and per-vertex normals all run on the GPU.

8 1.00
0.0 m/s 9.8 m/s² 1.80×
Presets

Interaction: drag to orbit · right-drag to pan · wheel to zoom · shift-drag to grab the cloth and pull it. Turn on Tearing and pull hard to rip it; links break when stretched past the threshold. Presets and the grid size reset the cloth; Wind is set by each preset and can be changed.

20Path Tracer

A compute shader traces one Monte Carlo light path per pixel per frame through spheres, quads, boxes and planes with diffuse, metal, glass and emissive materials, and adds it to a running average. Light sampling with multiple importance sampling makes it converge faster; noise falls as more samples arrive, and the run stops on its own at the target sample count.

1024 5
0.0 EV off
40° 0.030 3.50
Scenes

Interaction: drag to orbit · right-drag to pan · wheel to zoom · click to focus on the point under the cursor (with depth of field on). Every camera or setting change restarts the accumulation; exposure and tone mapping do not. Caustics take many samples: the bright speckles are light focused through glass, and the firefly clamp hides them at the cost of a slightly darker image.

21MNIST: Train

A small convolutional network (two 5×5 convolutions with ReLU and max-pooling, then a dense layer; 5,994 weights) learns handwritten digits from 10,000 MNIST images, entirely in compute shaders: batch assembly, forward pass, softmax cross-entropy, backpropagation through every layer and SGD with momentum. After each epoch it is tested on 2,000 images it has never seen.

0.04 15 4

Controls: Start loads the data (1.6 MB) on first use and trains; Pause keeps the weights, Reset re-initializes them with the next seed. The default settings and first seed repeat the CPU run that produced the cached weights of example 22. Learning rate, batch size and shifts apply from the next mini-batch. Export weights downloads a .bin in the same format as data/mnist/weights.bin; Use in Recognize hands the current weights to example 22. Hover the charts for values. MNIST data: CC BY-SA 3.0, see data/mnist/LICENSE.md.

23N-Body Gravity (Barnes-Hut)

Up to 262,144 self-gravitating stars integrated with leapfrog. Brute force sums all N² pairs with workgroup-shared tiles; Barnes-Hut rebuilds an octree-like tree on the GPU every step (bounding box, Morton codes, radix sort, Karras radix tree, bottom-up centers of mass with atomic counters) and walks it with a fixed-size stack, treating distant groups as single masses. The energy readout shows how much accuracy θ trades for speed.

0.50 0.050 0.010 2×
Presets

Interaction: drag to orbit · right-drag to pan · wheel to zoom. θ is the Barnes-Hut opening angle (smaller is more accurate and slower), softening ε smooths close encounters, Speed is leapfrog steps per frame. Changing θ, ε or the mode restarts the energy baseline; presets and the body count reset the simulation. Brute force is limited to 32,768 bodies.

24Convolution Reverb

A live stereo signal convolved on the GPU with an impulse response of up to 6 seconds: uniformly partitioned overlap-save FFT convolution, with the input spectra kept in a frequency-domain delay line and all partitions multiply-accumulated in one dispatch per block. Impulse responses are generated procedurally (or upload your own WAV). An A/B switch compares the result with the browser's ConvolverNode.

2.2 s 22 ms 35% wet -10.5 dB
Space

Audio: nothing plays until you press Start or a source; the output is capped at 0.5 with a limiter and soft clip. Use headphones with the microphone (speakers feed back into a reverb). Latency is one block for the partition plus the GPU slack; the dry signal and the ConvolverNode are delayed by the same amount, so A/B compares like with like. Canvas: top left the IR waveform (left channel above, right below), bottom left its spectrogram (50 Hz-16 kHz, 0 to -90 dB) with the Schroeder energy decay (white), the -60 dB line and the measured RT60 (green); top right the latest input block (blue) and GPU wet block (orange); bottom right peak meters for input L/R, wet L/R and the final output (-60 to 0 dBFS).
